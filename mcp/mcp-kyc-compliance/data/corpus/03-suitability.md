# Product suitability

A product is suitable for a client if two conditions both hold.

First, the product's risk rating must not exceed the client's declared
risk profile: a conservative client may only be offered conservative
products, a moderate client may be offered conservative or moderate
products, and an aggressive client may be offered any product regardless
of its risk rating.

Second, the proposed investment must not raise the client's concentration
in a single sector above 25% of the client's total portfolio value. This
is intended to prevent a client's holdings from becoming overly
concentrated in one sector, even if each individual product offered would
otherwise be suitable on a standalone basis. If a client's exposure to a
sector is already at or above this threshold, further products in that
same sector should not be recommended until the client's overall
allocation is rebalanced.

Both conditions must hold for a product to be considered suitable; failing
either one is sufficient grounds to withhold the recommendation.
